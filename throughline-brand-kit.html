brand kit — v1, drawn from the one-pager

The visual language of noticing, not scoring.

This is what's already established in the Throughline one-pager, pulled out and named so it can be applied consistently — to onboarding, the AI layer's copy, and anything new we build. Nothing here is a new decision; it's the existing system made explicit.

Mark

One person, many dates, one shape emerging

The mark is a thread, not a logo in the badge/icon sense — three dots joined by dashed connectors, the last one solid rose. It reads as a timeline that hasn't resolved into a line yet, which is the whole product thesis in one glyph.

Throughline

Use the mark and wordmark together at launch-facing moments (splash, share cards, footer). In-product, the dashed-connector motif can appear alone — see Motif, below.

Color

Paper, ink, and two signal colors

Warm and low-saturation on purpose — this is a private journal, not a dashboard. Rose and sage are the only two colors that carry meaning; everything else is neutral.

Paper
#EFE8D8
App background. Never pure white.
Paper card
#F8F3E6
Cards, entries, raised surfaces. A shade lighter than paper — no drop shadow needed.
Ink
#23241F
Primary text, hairlines, mark outlines.
Rose
#B25A68
Pre-date status, "before" moments, italic kickers. Warmth, not warning.
Sage
#5F6F4E
Dating status, growth/pattern moments, positive-but-quiet confirmation.
Muted
#5C5D52
Timestamps, captions, secondary labels.

Rose and sage are never a red/green good–bad pair — they mark pipeline stage (pre-date vs. dating), not a verdict on the person or the date. Don't let them drift into a traffic-light system.

Type

Fraunces for feeling, Inter for function

Two families, clearly distinct roles. Fraunces (a soft, literary serif) carries anything that should feel like it was written, not generated — headlines, italic kickers, the AI's own observations. Inter handles everything that needs to be read quickly and neutrally — body copy, timestamps, buttons, form labels.

Fraunces · display
Every date leaves a trace.
Headlines, section titles. Weight 500, sizes 28–54px depending on context.
Fraunces · italic kicker
a concept for a dating journal
Section eyebrows, stage labels ("Before" / "After"), and — importantly — the AI layer's noticing statements ("You've mentioned intellectual equality 11 times"). Italic signals "an observation," never a fact.
Inter · body
How it felt, what you talked about, what you'd want again. Written while it's still fresh, attributed to that person automatically.
Body copy, entries, notes. Weight 400. Line length under ~65 characters for comfortable reading.
Inter · UI label
notes about them  ·  entries  ·  dating  ·  stepped away
Sentence case always — no tracked-out all-caps labels. Weight 500 for anything interactive.
Motif

Dashes connect, dots mark a moment

The dashed hairline is the connective tissue of the whole system — it's what turns a list of separate dates into a thread. Solid dots mark a completed entry; a hollow or faint dot marks something not yet real (a prospect, a step not yet taken).

This motif replaces badges, streak counters, and progress bars everywhere. Progress in Throughline looks like a thread getting longer, not a meter filling up.

Pre-date — arranging
Dating — one or more dates in
Prospect / stepped away — outside the pipeline
Voice

Noticing, not verdicts

This is the one place brand and product constraint are the same rule: every piece of copy, especially anything AI-generated, offers an observation the user can take or leave. It never delivers a score, a diagnosis, or a label for another person.

Avoid
Use instead
score"You are 87% compatible."
pattern"Here are three things you've consistently valued."
label"You're attracted to unhealthy people."
tension"There appears to be a tension between what you say you want and who you find yourself attracted to."
diagnosis"You're anxiously attached."
description"You've described feeling anxious after three of your last five dates."
gamified"🔥 7-date streak! Keep it going."
unhurried"You've logged 7 dates since you started paying attention."
Applying the system

Do / don't

Do

  • Use italic Fraunces to visually mark anything the app is "noticing," so it reads differently from the user's own writing
  • Let sage and rose mark pipeline stage only
  • Use the dashed-line motif for progress and connection
  • Keep labels in sentence case
  • Leave generous whitespace — this is a journal, it should feel unhurried

Don't

  • Introduce a percentage, star rating, or score as a visual pattern
  • Use emoji as a substitute for iconography (🔥📉🚩 read as competitor, not Throughline)
  • Add streaks, badges, or progress bars
  • Use rose/sage as a red/green good-bad signal
  • Reach for drop shadows or rounded SaaS-card chrome — flat, hairline-bordered surfaces only
