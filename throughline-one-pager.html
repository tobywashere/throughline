a concept for a dating journal

Every date leaves a trace.

Throughline is a place to write down how a date actually felt, minutes after it ends — so the small, honest details add up into something you can actually learn from.

one person, many dates, one shape emerging

The habit the app is built around

Two small moments of writing, one on each side of a date, are the entire mechanism. Everything else in the app is built to make those two moments effortless and to make sense of them afterward.

Before

Add the person

A name, how you met, maybe a first impression. Takes under a minute, and gives every date that follows somewhere to attach to.

After

Log the date

How it felt, what you talked about, what you'd want again. Written while it's still fresh, attributed to that person automatically.

Over time

Watch the shape form

One date is an anecdote. Ten dates, logged the same honest way, start to look like a pattern you can actually name.

Entering the pipeline is its own moment

Prospect sits outside the pipeline on purpose — being interested in someone isn't the same as anything being underway. The pipeline itself only ever has two statuses, pre-date and dating, and someone can arrive at pre-date from two different places.

Path one

Met them first

A party, a mutual friend. Add them as a prospect — notes only, no pipeline status yet. When you actually start arranging a date with them, that's the moment they enter the pipeline as pre-date.

Path two

Date came first

A dating app, a setup — no prior record at all. Reporting the date starts with "who was this with," and adding a new person there drops them straight into dating — skipping prospect and pre-date, since a date already happened.

Once a date is reported, pre-date becomes dating — one status, whether it's the first date or the fifteenth. Stepping away moves someone to no longer seeing, off the board but reversible: if things pick back up, they return straight to dating, notes and entries intact.

Four screens, one thread running through them

Nothing here is a dashboard for its own sake. Each screen answers one question the last one couldn't.

Home — "where do things stand, in the pipeline?"

Home

The landing screen is the pipeline: a kanban-style board of everyone you're actively scheduling with or seeing, grouped as pre-date or dating. Prospects don't appear here — being interested in someone isn't the same as anything being in motion, so they stay in People until an actual date starts getting arranged. People you've stepped away from sit in a quiet strip beneath the board, since that's a reversible status, not a "closed" outcome.

Dates — "how did it actually go?"

Dates

Every date you've logged, in one running list. Each entry carries who it was with, how it felt in your own words, and a small set of tags — the ones that make it possible to compare a date in March to one in October without re-reading either.

People — "who is this, across every date?"

People

One thread per person, holding two different kinds of writing. Notes is a running, timestamped log, not a single block — jot something the day you meet someone, add more a month later, whether or not they're in the pipeline. Entries is the timeline of actual dates, one per date, each with its own structured reflection. A note can also attach to a specific past entry — something you remembered afterward — without touching that entry's original reflection fields. A prospect has notes and nothing else — no pipeline status, no entries — until a date starts getting arranged and they move into pre-date.

Journey — "what does this tell me about me?"

Journey

Zoomed all the way out, across everyone. Which traits keep showing up in dates that went well, which settings tend to flop, whether things have actually shifted since you started paying attention. The point isn't a score — it's a clearer sense of what you're actually looking for.

How the data actually connects

There are really three things underneath all four screens: a person, carrying a status; entries, one per date, each attributed to a person; and notes — short, timestamped, appendable at any time to either a person or a specific date entry. The Journey is nothing more than every entry, from every person, looked at together — notes add context along the way but aren't themselves the pattern-finding material.

Notes attach to a person or an entry, any time Entries only start once a date happens Journey reads across entries only

The bet this app is making

Most dating apps end at the date. Throughline starts there. If the small, honest write-up after each date is easy enough to actually do, the pattern-finding takes care of itself — you don't have to trust your memory of six months ago, because you already wrote it down.
